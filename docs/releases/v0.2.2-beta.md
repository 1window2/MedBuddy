## Status

- Candidate version: `0.2.2+23`
- Stable baseline: `v0.2.1-beta`
- Preparation branch: `beta/v0.2.2`
- Target tag: `v0.2.2-beta`

This document records the candidate boundary. The GitHub pre-release notes
carry the final commit identifiers and signed artifact checksums.

## Scope

v0.2.2 is the hardening line before a 1.0.0 release decision: every recorded
risk is either corrected or listed below as an open decision. It adds one user
feature, schedules for medications that are not taken every day, because
storing such directions as a daily count was a safety defect rather than a
missing convenience.

## Medication schedules

- A direction that is not daily is scheduled on its dose days instead of every
  day. Read from the frequency text: every N days ("격일", "2일 1회",
  "3일마다"), weekly ("주 1회", "매주"), every N weeks ("2주 1회", "격주"),
  N times a week ("주 3회", spread over the week from the start date), monthly
  ("월 1회", every 30 days) and named weekdays ("월요일, 목요일", "월수금").
  Today's schedule, the caregiver view, missed-dose alerts, the chat dose
  record and local reminders all use the same dose days. Until now such a
  medication was shown and reminded every day.
- A count per week or month is one dose on a dose day ("주 3회" was read as
  three doses a day). "8시간마다" is three doses a day (was four).
- Durations are read with their unit: "2주" is 14 days and "1개월" 30 days
  (were 2 and 1).
- The prescription analysis keeps a non-daily direction as written instead of
  reducing it to a number, the review screen marks such a medication, and its
  edit dialog states on which days doses are scheduled and how many dose days
  fall inside the total days. Changing the time slots no longer replaces the
  direction with a daily count.
- A medication saved from a pill photograph or registered manually can be
  given its dose days: every day, every other day, once a week or once a
  month. Manual registration states the resulting days before saving.
- The medication box shows the next dose day of a medication that is not taken
  every day, because such a medication is absent from today's schedule on its
  rest days.
- The medication box reads the medication period with the same rule as the
  schedule: a course of "8주" was shown as ending after eight days.
- Limits: "as needed" directions are scheduled as before; a monthly direction
  repeats every 30 days, not on a calendar date.
- The dose-day schedule is given only to an app that sends the `dose-days`
  client feature (0.2.2 and later). A released app keeps the daily schedule
  it had, because it switches a slot's reminder off on the server when today's
  list has no medication for that slot. Caregiver missed-dose alerts use the
  dose days for every app.

## Security

- The push notification of a server-written dose record carries no medication
  names.
- The two display names of a link are returned only to the participant who set
  them.
- Daily quotas per account and per IP address on the AI-backed routes and on
  hospital search; hospital and pharmacy providers are queried with a
  coordinate rounded to about 100 m.
- One account may hold 300 saved medications and 20 active links as a patient
  and as a caregiver; further ones are refused.
- A chat stream is closed when the ID token it was opened with expires, and
  when its link is no longer active.
- A session ended by the server cancels the account's local reminders and
  deletes the device push token; an action taken while signed out is not
  applied to a different account.
- Removing a notification from the inbox erases its stored content.
- The Android app no longer declares microphone or shared-storage access. The
  camera plugin contributed `RECORD_AUDIO`, `WRITE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE` and,
  through the merger, `READ_EXTERNAL_STORAGE` for video recording, which the
  app does not do.
- Third-party GitHub Actions (`subosito/flutter-action`,
  `google-github-actions/auth`, `google-github-actions/setup-gcloud`) are
  pinned to a commit instead of a movable tag; a test keeps them pinned.
- Errors raised by parsing libraries are no longer returned to the client.

## Reminders

- On Android 14 and later, where exact alarms are denied by default, the
  schedule screen and the settings show a notice with one button that opens
  the system "Alarms & reminders" screen. After the permission is granted,
  already scheduled reminders are replaced by exact ones on the next refresh
  or when the app returns to the foreground. Nothing changes for a user who
  does nothing. `USE_EXACT_ALARM` is not declared.
- After a force-stop the app no longer trusts the plugin's list of pending
  alarms and re-arms each future date once; a course without a known duration
  fills the 14-day window instead of one day at a time; the background worker
  takes doses recorded offline into account before re-arming a slot.
- Switching a reminder off while changing its time keeps the new time.
- Reminder saves, cancellation and reconciliation are serialized. A stale read
  or a late response from a closed session cannot overwrite a newer setting or
  re-arm the old account's reminders. Moving the time earlier than now keeps
  today's still-future old alarm; an unchanged snooze is preserved.
- Background refresh projects the current account's durable pending doses in
  order, including undo and retake, without uploading them or activating an
  account. A changing receipt, midnight boundary or unreadable queue leaves
  reminders intact for retry; partial completion still keeps the slot reminder.

## Reliability

- The weekly catalogue refresh keeps its schedule in the database (migration
  `c5e1a7f3b902`); a redeploy no longer starts a new seven-day wait.
- A caregiver missed-dose alert is never sent before the patient's own
  reminder time for the slot plus 30 minutes, whatever deadline was saved.
- A health recommendation never asks the model about a medication the server
  has no information for; such medications are named in a server-written
  caution instead.
- A partial AI summary of a medication is retried after a day instead of being
  kept until its source document changes.
- A caregiver linked after a slot's completion alert was sent receives the
  alert when the slot is completed again that day.
- The caregiver deadline dialog proposes the slot time plus one hour and
  refuses a deadline earlier than the slot time.
- Sign-out makes one bounded attempt (four seconds) to upload doses recorded
  offline before the session ends; what could not be sent stays queued for the
  same account.
- The server delivers the reason of a refused chat stream as a close code to
  an app that sends the `chat-close-codes` client feature; older apps keep the
  refusal before accept.
- The app reads the chat stream's close codes: it reconnects with a fresh
  token on 4440, stops on 4404, refreshes the token once on 4401 before ending
  the session, and waits as told on 4429.
- The server logs the link and the close code of every refused chat stream;
  the access log alone shows each refusal before accept as the same 403.
- A chat whose link was removed by the other participant says so: the input
  is replaced by a notice and the status reads "link ended". Before, the chat
  showed "disconnected" and a failed send asked to try again.
- While selecting chat messages, a tap on a message selects it; only the
  checkbox reacted before.
- Cancelling or replacing prescription input invalidates delayed picker, OCR
  and analysis results. App-owned photo cleanup waits for readers; gallery
  originals are preserved. Manual/analysed saves cannot continue with photo
  attachment, another save or refresh after their account state is cleared.
  Local cancellation does not undo a write already accepted by the server.
- Settings reads, manual saves and device-language updates are serialized.
  Device-language mode synchronizes at startup, on locale changes and on
  resume; failed saves retain local edits, and an old account's late response
  cannot change the new account's language.
- Unlinking requires explicit confirmation of stopped schedule sharing and
  deletion of that link's notification settings and shared chat history.
  Medications and dose records remain. Cancellation, repeated approval and
  stale account/screen confirmations do not send an additional unlink request.
- SQLite no longer reuses deleted medication/chat IDs issued under the new
  AUTOINCREMENT schema. Existing IDs stay unchanged; IDs deleted before the
  migration cannot be reconstructed, so review pending device work before
  rollout. Late push failures disable only the registration version observed
  for that send, preserving transferred or re-registered tokens.
- Missing push tokens and failed notification-channel initialization are
  retryable. Backend cleanup continues closing remaining resources after a
  worker failure; regression tests isolate developer database and Redis state.

## Compatibility

- The server remains compatible with the 0.2.0 and 0.2.1 apps. Schedule rows
  gain three fields (`dose_cycle_days`, `dose_cycle_offsets`,
  `dose_cycle_anchor`), the alarm disable route accepts an optional time, the
  chat stream uses the new close code 4440 for an expired credential, and the
  request header `X-MedBuddy-Client-Features` selects the dose-day schedule and
  the close codes of a refused chat stream.
- Alarm disable compatibility: no body or `{}` preserves the existing time;
  an hour alone sets minutes to `00`; minutes without an hour is rejected.
- Migrations: `c5e1a7f3b902` adds the empty `catalog_refresh_state` table;
  `d19e7c4b2a60` adds SQLite ID preservation and the push registration-version
  column. `f2c8a6d4e901` is their no-op merge and the required integrated head.
  Existing migration histories are preserved. Stop services, back up and
  rehearse upgrade/rollback before changing the serving schema; see
  [Production Deployment](../Production%20Deployment.md).

## Verification

October 11 documentation integration: both overall UML sources passed local
PlantUML 1.2026.8 rendering; each PNG's embedded source matches its `.puml`.
All 58 checked local documentation/test links resolve, and the documentation
diff passes whitespace/conflict checks. Focused backend verification passed
288 tests and 16 subtests; the later alarm-disable compatibility rerun passed
25 tests (overlapping coverage, not an additional full-suite total).

October 12 final state: the backend suite passes 1,731 tests and 68 subtests
(2 skipped), Flutter analysis is clean and 1,944 Flutter tests pass; both CI
workflows, including the PostgreSQL migration job and the release APK build
test, pass on the final commit.

The signed build was installed over 0.2.1 on a Galaxy Note10+ (Android 12) and
on an Android 14 emulator, both against the production server, which runs the
backend of this branch. Checked there: the update keeps the session and the
data; dose-day schedules; manual registration with dose days; exact reminders,
their recovery after a force-stop and after a reboot (emulator), the reminder
at its minute with the app not running and both of its actions; the widget;
the exact-alarm notice (emulator); chat, its message deletion and the notice
after a link is removed; the caregiver's view and the missed-dose alert with
its actions; the guided camera and the pharmacy map. The released 0.2.0 and
0.2.1 apps keep working against the new server.

Not checked on a device: prescription analysis of a real prescription with
this version, two physical phones, more than three sessions on one link, a
transient FCM failure, behaviour right after midnight and through the 12-hour
worker, and Android 16. The dated records are in the TODO.
